1. What Loxley is

Loxley organises collective consumer boycotts. Members propose a boycott by naming a company and an issue and writing what the company has to change; a moderator reads it and it runs. Joining is the whole commitment, and it counts from the day it is made. Choosing not to buy something, and saying publicly why, is lawful expression.

3. Signing in

Reading Loxley requires nothing. Joining a boycott or submitting a proposal requires Sign in with Apple. Loxley receives only an opaque identifier that Apple generates for this app; it requests no name and no email address and is never sent either.

4. Moderation

A submission names its target by picking a company from a register of SEC filers and an issue from a fixed list. Neither is free text, so the campaign that results is visible to other users immediately. Everything you write about it - the title, the case, the demands - is reviewed by a person before any other user can see it. Submissions are also screened automatically at the point of submission, and content matching prohibited terms is rejected.

You can report any content using the flag on the item, and block any member. Reports are reviewed and acted on within 24 hours. Content that breaks these terms is removed, and repeat or serious breaches result in permanent suspension without notice.

Moderation decisions are made at Loxley's discretion. There is no obligation to publish any submission.

5. Your account

Your account is an identifier and nothing else. You are responsible for what is submitted from it. You may delete it at any time from Account > Delete my account, which removes it and all associated content permanently.

6. The numbers in the app are estimates

Loxley does not have access to any company's sales data. Impact figures are models built from publicly stated assumptions, which are disclosed in full inside the app under "How We Calculate This", combined with what members voluntarily log. They are estimates of collective intent, not measurements of any company's actual revenue, and must not be presented as such.

Projected lines on charts show what full participation would look like. They are targets, not measurements.
